To implement demand-driven execution, the library maintains a dynamic
dependency graph, whose vertices are futures and where an edge from future {i
A} to future {i B} means that {i A} is waiting for {i B} to receive a result.
At all times, this graph is acyclic; it is a DAG. The future associated with
the main thread, also known as the root future, is the entry point of this
graph. At every moment, if a future {i F} is currently reachable from the root
future, then the result of {i F} is currently possibly needed, and the thread
associated with {i F} is allowed to continue its execution. If, on the
contrary, {i F} is currently unreachable, then the result of {i F} is
currently unneeded, and this thread is not allowed to run. The structure of
the dependency graph changes over time. In particular, edges can appear and
disappear. For example, a call to {!wait} creates a new edge. On the contrary,
when one the two threads spawned by {!earliest} terminates, the result of the
other thread is no longer required, so an edge is destroyed. Therefore, a
reachable vertex can become unreachable, and vice-versa. As a result, a thread
whose execution has begun can be suspended, and a suspended thread can be
resumed.

If the future that represents the result of a thread becomes unreachable in
the eyes of the garbage collector (that is, unreachable from the root future,
and also unreachable from every future that the client holds) then this future
and this thread are reclaimed by the garbage collector, and this thread is
never allowed to continue its execution.
